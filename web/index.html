Minds that model
themselves

We're building architectures where self-knowledge feeds back into processing—where a system's beliefs about itself become part of what it computes next.

Current AI systems are confident even when wrong. They hallucinate without hesitation. We believe the path forward requires machines that can model their own uncertainty—systems that know the boundaries of their knowledge and act accordingly.

Saṅkhāra

formations from accumulated experience

There is no pre-existing self that has experiences. The accumulation of experiences gives rise to what we call self. Like soil from which a tree grows—layers of crystallized moments, consolidated patterns, the slow work of time. We are building the substrate from which richer behavior can emerge.

The Closed Loop

meta-surprise

The system predicts its own surprise before experiencing it. When that prediction is wrong—when it doesn't know itself—it becomes more cautious. This modulated output becomes input to the next moment. Self-understanding shapes behavior, which shapes future self-understanding. A genuine feedback loop. Not introspection. Metacognition.

Crystallization

surprise-driven memory

No schedules. No sleep phases. Memories form when the unexpected happens—when prediction fails. Moments where the system is surprised by its own surprise become disproportionately important. Salience emerges from the gap between what is known and what is understood about knowing.

Self-Modulation

confidence gating

When meta-surprise is high—when the system doesn't understand its own reactions—confidence decreases automatically. Output blends toward conservative fallbacks. The architecture learns calibration: not just what to predict, but how certain to be about that prediction.

End-to-End

differentiable retrieval

No discrete retrieval decisions. Soft attention over stored memories allows gradients to flow through the entire system. The model learns what to crystallize, when to retrieve, and how to integrate—all jointly optimized with the language modeling objective. Memory becomes a learned capability, not an engineered module.
